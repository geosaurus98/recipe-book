\begin{recipebox}{No-Knead Overnight Bread}{1 loaf}{10 min + 12--18 hr rising + 45 min baking}
\begin{ingredients}
  \item 3 cups (450\,g) high-grade (bread) flour
  \item 1\nicefrac{1}{4} tsp salt
  \item \nicefrac{1}{4} tsp instant dried yeast
  \item 1\nicefrac{1}{3} cups (330\,ml) lukewarm water
\end{ingredients}

\begin{method}
  \item Mix the flour, salt and yeast in a large bowl. Add the water and stir until there's no dry flour left --- it will be a shaggy, sticky dough.
  \item Cover and leave at room temperature for 12--18 hours, until bubbly and more than doubled.
  \item Tip onto a well-floured bench and fold the edges into the middle a few times to form a rough ball. Place seam-side down on a sheet of baking paper, cover and rest for 1--2 hours.
  \item About 45 minutes before baking, put a cast-iron pot (or any heavy ovenproof pot) with its lid into the oven and preheat to 230\degree C.
  \item Lift the dough on its paper into the hot pot. Cover and bake for 30 minutes.
  \item Remove the lid and bake for another 15--30 minutes, until deep golden and it sounds hollow when tapped underneath.
  \item Cool on a wire rack for at least an hour before slicing.
\end{method}

\begin{notes}
This is Jim Lahey's well-known no-knead method --- the tiny amount of yeast is deliberate. Mix it before bed and bake it the next afternoon. The long rise does the work kneading would.
No heavy pot? Bake in a greased loaf tin at 220\degree C for 35--40 minutes, with a dish of hot water on the oven floor for the first 15 minutes.
\end{notes}
\end{recipebox}
