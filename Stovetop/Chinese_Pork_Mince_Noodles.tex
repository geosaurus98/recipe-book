% Chinese Pork Mince with Noodles ("Chinese Bolognese") — Stovetop. From RecipeTin Eats, scaled to 4.
\begin{recipebox}{Chinese Pork Mince with Noodles (``Chinese Bolognese'')}{4}{10 min prep + 10 min}

Savoury, slightly spicy pork mince over thick noodles with fresh cucumber, based
on Zha Jiang Mian. Scaled up to 500\,g mince for four, with edamame for extra
protein.

\begin{ingredients}
  \item[] \textbf{Sauce}
  \item \nicefrac{1}{2} cup chicken stock
  \item 1\nicefrac{1}{2} tbsp chilli bean sauce (doubanjiang)
  \item 1\nicefrac{1}{2} tbsp hoisin sauce (or Chinese sweet bean sauce)
  \item 1\nicefrac{1}{2} tbsp dark soy sauce
  \item 1 tbsp Chinese black vinegar (or balsamic vinegar)
  \item 1 tsp sugar
  \item[] \textbf{Stir-fry}
  \item 800\,g fresh udon or thick white noodles
  \item 1 tbsp oil
  \item \nicefrac{1}{2} onion, finely chopped
  \item 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  \item 1\nicefrac{1}{2} tsp grated ginger
  \item 500\,g pork mince
  \item 1 cup frozen shelled edamame
  \item[] \textbf{To serve}
  \item 2 cucumbers, cut into batons
  \item Coriander leaves and chilli sauce
\end{ingredients}

\begin{method}
  \item \textbf{Sauce.} Mix the sauce ingredients in a bowl.
  \item \textbf{Noodles.} Prepare the noodles as the packet says.
  \item \textbf{Aromatics.} Heat the oil in a wok or large frying pan over
        medium-high heat. Cook the onion for 1 minute, then the garlic and ginger
        for 1 minute, until the onion is translucent.
  \item \textbf{Pork.} Add the pork and cook, breaking it up, until browned. Add the
        edamame.
  \item \textbf{Simmer.} Add the sauce and cook for 3--5 minutes, until most of it
        has reduced.
  \item \textbf{Serve.} Divide the noodles between bowls, top with the pork and
        coriander, and serve the cucumber on the side. Mix it all up as you eat,
        with plenty of chilli sauce.
\end{method}

\begin{notelist}
  \item No chilli bean sauce? Use 2 tsp miso plus 2 tsp sriracha.
  \item Thick flour noodles are traditional, but any noodles work. Fresh flour
        noodles are heavy, so 800\,g isn't as much as it sounds.
\end{notelist}
