\begin{recipebox}{Smoky Chicken, Black Bean \& Red Lentil Stew}{4}{\textasciitilde6--7 hr (slow cooker, low)}

A dump-and-go slow cooker stew with smoky paprika, cumin and a Worcestershire
backbone. The red lentils mostly disappear into the sauce, making it thicker
and more filling without it tasting like obvious lentil punishment. Makes 4
large servings at roughly 45--50\,g protein each.

\begin{ingredients}
  \item[] \textbf{Slow cooker base}
  \item 1 onion, diced
  \item 2 carrots, diced or thinly sliced
  \item 2--3 garlic cloves, crushed
  \item \nicefrac{1}{2} cup dry red lentils, rinsed
  \item 1 can crushed tomatoes
  \item 1\nicefrac{1}{2} cups chicken stock
  \item 2 tsp smoked paprika
  \item 2 tsp ground cumin
  \item 1 tsp dried oregano
  \item \nicefrac{1}{2} tsp chilli powder (or to taste)
  \item 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  \item 600\,g chicken breast, left whole
  \item[] \textbf{Last 20--30 minutes}
  \item 1 can black beans, drained \& rinsed
  \item 350\,g frozen mixed vegetables or corn (optional)
  \item[] \textbf{To finish \& serve}
  \item \nicefrac{1}{2} tsp salt and \nicefrac{1}{4} tsp pepper
  \item 4 cups cooked rice
  \item Squeeze of lime or splash of vinegar (optional)
\end{ingredients}

\begin{method}
  \item \textbf{Build the base.} Add the onion, carrots, garlic, rinsed lentils,
        tomatoes, stock, spices and Worcestershire sauce to the slow cooker.
        Stir well so the lentils are spread through the liquid, not clumped
        on the bottom.
  \item \textbf{Add the chicken.} Lay the whole chicken breasts on top. They
        don't need to be submerged.
  \item \textbf{Slow cook.} Cook on Low for 6--7 hours. If you're home,
        4--5 hours is enough for whole breasts and keeps them juicier.
  \item \textbf{Shred.} Lift out the chicken, shred it with two forks, and stir
        it back into the sauce.
  \item \textbf{Add the beans \& veg.} Stir in the drained black beans and any
        frozen vegetables or corn. Cook for a final 20--30 minutes with the lid
        \emph{off} (switch to High if needed) so the sauce reduces while
        everything heats through.
  \item \textbf{Adjust \& serve.} Too thin $\rightarrow$ mash a few of the
        beans against the side of the pot and simmer a little longer. Too thick
        $\rightarrow$ loosen with a splash of water or stock. Season with salt
        and pepper, add a squeeze of lime or splash of vinegar if using, then
        serve over rice.
\end{method}

\begin{variationbox}{No-tomato version}
Replace the crushed tomatoes with an extra 1\nicefrac{1}{2} cups of stock, and
add 1 tbsp tomato paste, BBQ sauce or salsa for flavour. The tomatoes are the
better fit here, though --- use them if you have them.
\end{variationbox}

\begin{variationbox}{Instant Pot (pressure)}
Layer as above, but \emph{don't} stir in the tomatoes --- pour them on top of
everything else and leave them, and add an extra \nicefrac{1}{2} cup of stock.
Lentils and thick tomato sauce on the base are a classic burn-error trigger.
Finish the beans and veg on Saut\'e.
\end{variationbox}

\begin{notelist}
  \item \textbf{Keep the chicken whole} for the long cook, then shred at the
        end --- it stays juicier than diced breast would over 6--7 hours.
  \item \textbf{Start wet, finish thick.} The red lentils soak up a lot of
        liquid over the long cook, so the base starts deliberately loose ---
        it's easy to reduce at the end, but a dry pot can catch. The stew
        thickens again in the fridge, so loosen leftovers with a splash of
        water when reheating.
  \item \textbf{Beans go in late} so they hold their shape rather than turning
        to mush.
  \item \textbf{Grated carrot} works too if you'd rather it melted into the
        sauce instead of staying as visible pieces.
\end{notelist}

\end{recipebox}
